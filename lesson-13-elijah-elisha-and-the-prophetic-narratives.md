### ★ Signature Game: The Carmel Showdown · +5 bonus XP

**Cast.** Elijah · prophets of Baal · Ahab · the people · narrator.

**Run it.** Perform 1 Kings 18:20-39 in about 8 minutes. The audience scores each moment on one question: *who is God here?*

**Syncretism check.** Mark each line **S** (syncretism) or **F** (faithful worship of the LORD):

1. "We worship the LORD on the Sabbath and Baal for the harvest."
2. "The LORD alone governs fire and rain."
3. "Both gods help us in different seasons."

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1 S · 2 F · 3 S. Elijah's ministry opposes the blending of the LORD's worship with other practices.
</details>

## Level 4: Boss — The Misconception Buster 🧠 · 20 XP

The boss says:

> *"The Elijah and Elisha stories are about supernatural power for its own sake, impressive miracles with no ethical content."*

Defeat it in about 250 words. Your weapons are the lesson's five key takeaways:

- Mount Carmel stages a theological contest over who is God, not merely a demonstration of raw power.
- Elijah's despair and restoration at Horeb (1 Kings 19) shows prophetic ministry includes exhaustion as well as triumph.
- The Elisha cycle's miracles express God's compassion for the poor and vulnerable, not spectacle for its own sake.
- Naboth's vineyard (1 Kings 21) shows the prophetic tradition confronting political and economic injustice directly.
- Pentecostal and Reformed traditions each contribute real insight into how prophetic ministry continues today.

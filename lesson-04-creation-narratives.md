# Lesson 4: Creation Narratives (Genesis 1–2)

> **Unit II: Primeval and Patriarchal Narratives** · Game: **Build Seven Days** · Badge: **Image Bearer** · About 60 minutes of play

### ★ Signature Game: Whose Creation Is It? · +5 bonus XP

**Round 1: Build Seven Days.** Arrange the seven days of Genesis 1, then check the pairs.

| Day | What God makes or does |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Light |
| 2 | Sky and waters divided |
| 3 | Land, seas and plants |
| 4 | Sun, moon and stars |
| 5 | Sea creatures and birds |
| 6 | Land animals and humankind |
| 7 | Rest |

A common reading pairs the *framing* days with the *filling* days: 1↔4, 2↔5, 3↔6. Day 7 stands alone as creation's crown.

**Round 2: Spot the difference.** Genesis 1 set beside the Enuma Elish. Mark each statement **G** (Genesis 1) or **E** (Enuma Elish).

1. The world arises from the body of the chaos monster Tiamat.
2. God speaks and creation comes into being.
3. Creation is repeatedly declared good.
4. Creation is the by-product of conflict between the gods.
5. Humanity is made in the image of God.

## Level 4: Boss — The Misconception Buster 🧠 · 20 XP

The boss says:

> *"Genesis 1 is basically the Babylonian creation story with the names changed."*

Defeat it in about 250 words. Your weapons are the lesson's five key takeaways:

- Genesis 1's seven-day structure and refrains are deliberate literary craftsmanship, not incidental repetition.
- Genesis 1 makes pointed theological claims against its Ancient Near Eastern setting rather than simply borrowing from it.
- Imago Dei carries both functional (royal-priestly vocation) and relational (capacity for relationship) dimensions.
- Sabbath is creation's crown, not an afterthought, a rhythm of rest built into creation itself.
- The cultural mandate grounds human creativity and stewardship, not exploitation, of creation.

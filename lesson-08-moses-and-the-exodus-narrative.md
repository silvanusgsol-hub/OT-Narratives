# Lesson 8: Moses and the Exodus Narrative

> **Unit III: Exodus, Conquest, and Settlement** · Game: **Five Locked Doors** · Badge: **Name Bearer** · About 60 minutes of play

At the burning bush Moses argues with God five times. Each objection is a locked door. Your mission: open them with God's answers, then read the plagues, Passover and Sinai as one story of liberation and covenant.

## Level 4: Boss — The Misconception Buster 🧠 · 20 XP

The boss says:

> *"The ten plagues were simply displays of raw power meant to frighten Pharaoh into compliance."*

Defeat it in about 250 words. Your weapons are the lesson's five key takeaways:

- The burning bush theophany reveals both the divine name and the pattern of 'human inadequacy plus divine presence.'
- The plagues dismantle Egyptian religious claims one deity at a time, not merely Pharaoh's political will.
- Passover functions simultaneously as historical narrative and perpetual liturgical re-enactment.
- The Sinai covenant constitutes Israel as a covenant community, not merely a liberated population.
- Liberation theology and evangelical readings of Exodus each contribute real insight, with real points of caution.

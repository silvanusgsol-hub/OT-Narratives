### ★ Signature Game: The King's Choice · +5 bonus XP

**Choose Your Samuel (1 Samuel 8).** Read aloud in a group. At each fork, choose what to say, then read what the narrator reports.

1. The elders say: *give us a king like the nations.* What do you say first?
2. The LORD tells Samuel to listen and warn. What do you warn about?
3. The people insist anyway. What is the difference between a concession and an approval?

**Bathsheba, closely read.** Mark 2 Samuel 11 for scene and summary. The narrator keeps his verdict until the last line of the chapter. Why wait?

**House wordplay (2 Samuel 7).** Fill in the blanks: David wants to build a ____ for God; God promises to build a ____ for David.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

house; house. The Hebrew *bayit* means both temple and dynasty, so the covenant plays on a single word.
</details>

**Messianic trajectory.** On one long strip of paper, mark 2 Samuel 7, then add one later text that picks up the promise of an eternal house. The *anointed one* is the king, and the promised Messiah inherits the Davidic covenant.

## Level 4: Boss — The Misconception Buster 🧠 · 20 XP

The boss says:

> *"'A man after God's own heart' means David was morally exemplary throughout his life."*

Defeat it in about 250 words. Your weapons are the lesson's five key takeaways:

- Israel's demand for a king is presented as both a rejection of divine kingship and a divine accommodation.
- Saul's disobedience, not his lack of talent, is the narrative's explanation for his rejection.
- David's election subverts human expectation: 'the LORD looks on the heart' (1 Sam. 16:7).
- The Davidic covenant (2 Sam. 7) is both conditional (individual kings disciplined) and unconditional (the dynasty preserved).
- The Bathsheba narrative holds honest sin, real repentance, and genuine forgiveness together without minimizing any of them.
